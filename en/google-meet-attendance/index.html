How to take attendance in Google Meet

Last updated: 28 October 2026 · Italiano

If you teach, tutor or run meetings on Google Meet, sooner or later you need to know who was there, when they joined and how long they stayed. Meet does not always give you that, and it depends on the kind of account you have. This guide covers the three ways to do it, with the pros and cons of each, so you can pick the right one.

1. By hand, from the participant list

During the call, open the People panel and write the names down on a sheet or register. It works with any account and it is free.

The catch: you have to do it while you are teaching. If someone drops and rejoins because of a connection problem, or the class is large, mistakes are easy, and you have no exact times.

2. Google Meet's own attendance report

Some paid Google Workspace editions include attendance tracking. According to Google's help page, it is available to Workspace Essentials, Business Plus, the Enterprise editions, Education Plus and the Teaching and Learning Upgrade. It is not available on a regular free Gmail account.

How to turn it on

  • During the meeting, open Host controls and switch on Attendance tracking.
  • Or, in Google Calendar, open the event, go to the conference settings and tick the same option before the meeting.

What you get

After the meeting, the organizer receives an email with a Google Sheets file that has name, email, time joined, time left and duration. If someone joins and leaves several times, the report shows the total duration, not the time of each visit.

The catch: you need a paid Workspace edition and your domain admin can turn the feature off. The report arrives only after the meeting, so you cannot check who is in the call while you are teaching.

Source: Google Meet Help, "Track attendance". Editions and rules can change: always check the current page.

3. A Chrome extension: Appelo

If you do not have an edition that includes Google's report, or you want to see attendance in real time, you can use an extension. Appelo is the one I built: it reads the participant list on the Meet page during the call and records when each person joined and left.

  • Live roster: see who is in the call now, since when, and for how many minutes.
  • Rejoins handled: someone who leaves and comes back is counted once, with the right total time.
  • CSV export, free, which opens correctly in Excel (accents and Arabic included).
  • No special Workspace edition needed: it reads the Meet page you see, so it does not depend on Google's paid features.
  • With Pro (optional): class lists to find absent, late and partly present students, Excel export, a history of all your lessons, an attendance report over time and automatic export when the call ends.
  • Privacy: there is no Appelo server and no account. Data stays in your browser. Read the privacy policy.

Frequently asked questions

How do I export the Google Meet participant list to Excel?

Meet has no button to export the list while the call is running. You have two options: Google's attendance report (only on the editions that include it) or an extension like Appelo, which gives you a CSV file to open in Excel at the end of the lesson.

Does Appelo work with a free Gmail account?

It does not need a paid Workspace edition: it reads the participant list you see on the Meet page. It works in Chrome and Chromium-based browsers on a computer.

What if a student leaves and rejoins?

Appelo treats an absence as a real leave only if it lasts more than 15 seconds, and adds up the time of every visit. A connection drop does not distort the attendance.

Where is the data stored?

Only in your browser. Appelo does not send participant names to any server.

How much does Pro cost?

EUR 3.99 a month, EUR 29 a year or EUR 59 once. The free version has no time limit and keeps your last 3 meetings; see the pricing section.
